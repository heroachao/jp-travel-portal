What problem this tool solves

A list of places is not yet an itinerary. This planner turns trip length, pace, interests, and preferred regions into a day-by-day route, helping you spot too many hotel changes, long backtracking days, and an unrealistic number of bases before you book.

Example use case

A first-time visitor with ten nights can compare a classic Tokyo-Kyoto-Osaka route with a slower version that gives Kyoto an extra night and removes one rushed side trip.

Who this is for

  • First-time visitors choosing how many cities fit their available nights.
  • Independent travelers who want a route outline before comparing hotels and rail costs.
  • Couples or families deciding whether to travel slowly or change bases more often.

Common mistakes

  • Counting arrival and departure days as full sightseeing days.
  • Adding distant regions without allowing for check-out, station access, and luggage.
  • Changing hotels for a place that works better as a day trip.
  • Treating the generated outline as fixed when weather or reservations require a backup.

Frequently asked questions

How many Japan regions should I include?

Start with the number of usable full days, not a wish list. Fewer bases usually mean less check-in time and more flexibility; add another region only when its travel time still leaves a worthwhile stay.

Does the planner make bookings?

No. It creates an on-site planning outline. Check current transport, hotel availability, opening days, and reservation rules before paying.

Should I enter my arrival day as a sightseeing day?

Usually allow a lighter arrival block. Immigration, baggage, airport transfer, hotel access, fatigue, and an unfamiliar station can consume more time than expected.

Can I plan day trips?

Yes. Use a stable hotel base and treat nearby places as day trips when the return route is simple. Check the final comfortable return, not only the fastest outbound train.

What does travel pace change?

Pace affects how many major stops fit in one day and how much recovery time remains. A slower plan leaves room for meals, queues, weather, and unplanned discoveries.

What should I do with the result?

Confirm the route order, compare hotel areas, estimate intercity transport, and keep one lower-priority day that can move when weather or closures change.
