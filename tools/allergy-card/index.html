Food Allergy Phrase Card

Create clear English and Japanese food allergy phrases for restaurants and shops.

Output Phrase card Runs locally in your browser

What problem this tool solves

A translated ingredient name alone may not communicate severity, cross-contact risk, or the need to refuse a dish. This tool creates a clear bilingual phrase card to support a restaurant conversation, while making it explicit that a card cannot verify ingredients or guarantee a safe meal.

Example use case

A traveler with a serious peanut allergy can prepare a concise Japanese card before the trip, show it before ordering, and still ask staff whether ingredients and shared cooking equipment can be checked.

Who this is for

  • Travelers with food allergies who need a consistent phrase ready before ordering.
  • Vegetarian or dietary-restriction travelers explaining ingredients they cannot eat.
  • Parents preparing a simple communication aid for a child.

Common mistakes

  • Showing the card only after food has been served.
  • Assuming a translation proves that a kitchen can prevent cross-contact.
  • Listing too many preferences so the critical allergy is unclear.
  • Traveling without medication, an emergency plan, or professional medical advice appropriate to the condition.

Frequently asked questions

Does the phrase card guarantee an allergy-safe meal?

No. It is a communication aid only. Staff may be unable to verify every ingredient or prevent cross-contact, so make decisions based on the response and your own medical plan.

When should I show the card?

Show it before ordering and allow time for staff to ask the kitchen. Do not wait until the dish arrives.

Should severity be included?

Yes. State the important allergy and severity clearly. Keep the message concise so a preference is not confused with a serious medical restriction.

Can the card cover cross-contact?

It can ask about shared oil, grills, utensils, or preparation areas, but it cannot confirm kitchen practice. If staff cannot verify safety, choose another option.

Can I rely on English menus?

No. Translated menus may shorten descriptions and omit sauces, stock, garnishes, or processing details. Ask directly when an ingredient matters.

What else should I prepare?

Carry prescribed medication, follow your clinician's advice, know how to seek emergency help, and research suitable food options near each hotel.
