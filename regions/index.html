Quick answer

Choose Japan regions by season, trip length, and transport effort. Tokyo and Kansai are easiest for first-time routes, while Hokkaido, Kyushu, Okinawa, and Chubu work best when the trip has a clear reason to go there.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing regions only by famous photos without checking transport time.
  • Adding a distant region for one night.
  • Treating Hokkaido or Okinawa as quick side trips from Tokyo.
  • Ignoring season when planning mountains, islands, or rural buses.
  • Choosing where to stay without checking the station or airport used most often.

FAQ

Practical questions before you book.

Which Japan region is best for a first trip?

Tokyo and Kansai are the easiest first combination because transport is frequent and there are many guides, hotels, and food options.

Which region is best in winter?

Hokkaido is the strongest winter region for snow scenery and ski access, while Kansai and Tokyo are easier for milder city travel.

What should every region page link to?

Every region page should link to the Region Finder, relevant itineraries, and transport pages for the main arrival routes.

Should I visit Okinawa on a first Japan trip?

Okinawa is excellent for beaches and slower travel, but it usually needs separate flight planning and more time than a quick city add-on.

How do I compare Tokyo and Kansai?

Tokyo is best for neighborhoods, shopping, food, and day trips. Kansai is best for Kyoto, Osaka, Nara, temples, and classic cultural stops.

Can one trip cover every major Japan region?

Not well. A stronger plan usually chooses two or three regions and leaves deeper northern, southern, or island routes for a later trip.
