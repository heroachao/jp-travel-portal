Best planning use

Use Tokyo as a neighborhood-led city, not as one giant checklist. First-time visitors usually get better days by pairing one large anchor such as Asakusa, Shinjuku, Ueno, Shibuya, Ginza, or Roppongi with nearby food, shopping, and evening views.

Transport and pacing

Plan around rail lines and station exits. Tokyo transfers can be easy on paper but tiring with luggage, rain, or late-night crowds, so every Tokyo route should include a realistic return line and one nearby backup.

Tokyo FAQ

How many nights should I spend in Tokyo?

Most travelers should give Tokyo enough nights to avoid treating it as a rushed transfer stop. Start with the recommended itinerary above, then adjust by season and arrival airport.

Is Tokyo good for a first Japan trip?

It can be, but the fit depends on transport simplicity, weather, and whether the region matches your main reason for visiting Japan.

What is the biggest planning mistake here?

The common mistake is adding too many distant stops without checking station access, luggage movement, and how much time each transfer removes from the day.

Should I rent a car in Tokyo?

Use public transport for rail-friendly cities and major routes. Consider a car only when the specific area, season, parking, and driving comfort make it clearly useful.

What should I check before booking?

Confirm hotel location, arrival airport, train or bus frequency, weather patterns, luggage plan, and whether the key sights need reservations or seasonal timing.
