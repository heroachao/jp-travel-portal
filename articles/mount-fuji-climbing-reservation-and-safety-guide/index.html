Mount Fuji Climbing: Reservation, Trail, Hut, and Safety Guide

Choose a Mount Fuji trail, verify the current climbing season and entry system, book the correct mountain hut, plan trailhead transport, and avoid unsafe overnight climbing.

Quick answer

Choose one of Mount Fuji's four official trails before paying any fee or booking a hut. Verify the current opening dates, trail-specific registration or reservation, entry restrictions, hiking fee, trailhead access, weather, volcanic information, and mountain-hut reservation on the official Mount Fuji climbing site. A trail entry registration is not a mountain-hut booking.

For 2026, the official site lists scheduled opening from July 1 for the Yoshida and Subashiri trails and July 10 for Fujinomiya, Gotemba, and the summit rim, with closing dates and facilities subject to weather and remaining snow. It also states current common entry restrictions and a hiking fee. These details are annual: travelers for another season must use that year's official page.

If you only do one thing: match the reservation, hut, bus, and descent to the same named trail.

Who this guide is for

This guide is for first-time Mount Fuji climbers, overseas visitors comparing the Yoshida, Subashiri, Gotemba, and Fujinomiya trails, and travelers deciding whether to use a mountain hut. It is not a fitness clearance, weather forecast, medical recommendation, or permission to climb outside the official season.

Mount Fuji exceeds 3,000 meters and presents altitude, cold, wind, darkness, volcanic terrain, weather, and crowd risk. The official site says thorough preparation is necessary and warns against climbing outside the designated season.

Choose the trail before the booking

The four trails differ in trailhead elevation, distance, terrain, crowding, number of huts, transport, and descent. The popular Yoshida route has many huts and separate ascent and descent sections, but popularity does not make it effortless. Gotemba is much longer; Fujinomiya has a high trailhead and steep sections; Subashiri includes forest before joining the upper Yoshida route.

Read the official comparison, current map, and trail-specific guide. Record the trail color, ascent, descent, hut name, and bus stop. Climbers have become lost by descending onto a different trail from the one expected.

Do not choose by summit sunrise alone. Choose by fitness, experience, transport, hut availability, weather, and the safest descent.

Understand registration, restrictions, and fees

The official site directs climbers to trail-specific entry registration and payment systems. In 2026 it describes common restriction hours from 2 p.m. to 3 a.m. for climbers not staying in a mountain hut, plus route-specific procedures and limits. The Yoshida Trail also has a capacity system and advance reservation process. Rules can differ by prefecture and year.

Use the official link for the chosen trail. Save the required proof offline and bring the same identity or device required by the system. Check refund, change, group, same-day, and no-show rules.

Payment or registration does not override a closure, unsafe weather, volcanic warning, lack of equipment, or medical condition. It also does not reserve accommodation.

Book the correct mountain hut

Official guidance says Mount Fuji huts require advance booking, operate only in season, provide basic shared accommodation, and generally do not have bathing or washing facilities. Meals and dietary accommodation vary. Check the exact hut's trail, altitude, check-in time, meal plan, sleeping arrangement, payment, cancellation, toilet, water, and emergency information.

A hut helps break the climb and can support acclimatization, but it does not guarantee sleep or remove altitude risk. Choose a hut that matches the group's pace rather than the highest available bed.

Avoid the unsafe practice of climbing through the night without hut accommodation simply to reach sunrise. Follow current official restrictions and use a model plan suited to the route.

Plan trailhead access and private-car restrictions

Each trail has a different fifth station and transport route. During peak periods, private-car restrictions can require parking at a designated area and using a shuttle. For 2026, the official season page lists a Fuji Subaru Line restriction period for Yoshida access, but the exact dates, bus timetable, parking, and road status must be checked every year.

Build the trip from the final bus backward. Add parking, shuttle queue, entry procedure, toilet, food, equipment check, and altitude acclimatization. The official Yoshida model plan includes time at the fifth or sixth station before climbing.

Do not leave large luggage at an uncertain trailhead facility. Use the hotel, locker, or delivery arrangement confirmed for the date.

Prepare for altitude, weather, darkness, and cold

Mount Fuji weather can change rapidly and the summit can be much colder and windier than the city. Check JMA, the official climbing site, volcanic information, and trail status. Carry proper layers, waterproof protection, sturdy footwear, headlamp, spare power, water, food, cash where needed, and the route map.

Climb slowly, use planned breaks, and recognize altitude-illness symptoms. Descend if symptoms worsen and follow staff or rescue instructions. The summit is optional; returning safely is the objective.

Darkness, congestion, loose volcanic gravel, and mixed ascent/descent traffic require attention. Keep to the official trail and do not create shortcuts. Review travel insurance and emergency assistance before the climb, including hiking altitude, rescue, medical care, exclusions, and required contact procedures. Insurance does not make an unsafe climb acceptable, but uncertainty about coverage should not be discovered during an emergency.

Use a two-day sequence that protects the descent

A common plan reaches a booked hut on day one, rests, continues under the official rules, and descends with enough daylight and transport margin. The best sunrise point may be the hut or trail rather than the summit when crowds and weather make the final climb unsafe.

Protect the descent. It is long, tiring, and can finish on a different surface and route. Carry enough water and know where facilities end. Keep the day after the climb light rather than booking a long transfer or international departure.

If weather closes the route, use a lower-elevation Fuji-area plan. A reservation does not justify climbing into a warning.

What to verify on official sources

Verify current opening and closure, route map, entry registration, hiking fee, restriction hours, daily limit, hut, trailhead bus, private-car restriction, parking, equipment, weather, volcanic alert, first aid, toilets, water, waste, emergency contacts, and descent.

Use the Official Website for Mt. Fuji Climbing and the linked prefectural system. Old annual articles, booking resellers, and social videos do not establish current access.

FAQ

Do I need a reservation to climb Mount Fuji?

Current entry registration, payment, restrictions, and reservations differ by trail and year. Use the official system linked for the chosen trail.

Does the Mount Fuji entry registration include a mountain hut?

No. Mountain huts require a separate advance booking with the correct hut on the chosen trail.

Can I climb Mount Fuji outside the official season?

The official site says trails to the summit are closed outside the designated season and that off-season climbing is extremely dangerous.

Which Mount Fuji trail is easiest?

No route is easy for everyone. Compare distance, elevation, terrain, huts, transport, crowds, and descent using the current official trail guide.

Can I drive to the Yoshida Trail fifth station?

Private-car restrictions can require parking and a shuttle during the season. Verify the current Fuji Subaru Line dates and access plan.

Is a summit sunrise guaranteed with a hut booking?

No. Weather, altitude, congestion, closures, and personal condition control the climb. A safe sunrise from the trail or hut may be the better result.
