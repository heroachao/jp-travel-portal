Quick answer: use the Subway Ticket only for subway-heavy Tokyo days

For a first Tokyo trip, buy the Tokyo Subway Ticket only when your next 24, 48, or 72 hours are mostly on Tokyo Metro and Toei Subway lines. Use Suica, PASMO, Welcome Suica, TOURIST PASMO, or another IC card when the route mixes JR, private railways, buses, airport trains, shopping, convenience stores, or uncertain last-minute changes. The pass can save money, but it is not a full Tokyo transport card.

Tokyo Metro's official Tokyo Subway Ticket page checked on July 18, 2026 lists adult prices of 1,000 yen for 24 hours, 1,500 yen for 48 hours, and 2,000 yen for 72 hours. It says the ticket covers all Tokyo Metro and Toei Subway lines, starts from first use, and is only available to short-term visitors entering Japan. The same official page says the 24-hour ticket saves money from at least six rides, the 48-hour ticket from at least five rides per 24 hours, and the 72-hour ticket from at least four rides per 24 hours.

Keep an IC card even if you buy the pass. Tokyo Metro says separate fares are needed when you transfer to another company's railway line, and the Tokyo Subway Ticket alone does not get you to either Narita or Haneda Airport. For most first-time visitors, the right answer is not pass or IC card. It is IC card as the default, plus a Subway Ticket only for a deliberately subway-heavy block.

Who this guide is for

This guide is for travelers deciding whether a Tokyo Subway Ticket is worth buying before or just after arrival. It is most useful if you are staying in Shinjuku, Ginza, Asakusa, Ueno, Akasaka, Roppongi, Nihonbashi, Ikebukuro, or another area with strong Tokyo Metro or Toei access, and you are trying to compare savings against route friction.

It is also for families, first-time visitors, and budget travelers who do not yet understand the difference between a subway pass and a stored-value IC card. If you already use route-planning apps confidently and your hotel is on JR or a private railway, the decision is simpler: keep an IC card as the main tool and buy a subway pass only after the exact next-day route proves it.

Decision table: Subway Ticket, Suica, PASMO, or a day pass

Start with coverage before price. The Tokyo Subway Ticket covers Tokyo Metro and Toei Subway. Suica and PASMO are stored-value IC cards that pay ordinary fares across many trains, subways, buses, and shops. A Tokyo Metro-only 24-hour ticket is cheaper, but it excludes Toei Subway. A one-day Metro plus Toei ticket is simple for one calendar day, but it is not the same 24-hour tourist product.

Step-by-step route test before buying

Step 1: put your hotel on the map and identify the actual station entrances you will use. A hotel that says "Shinjuku" or "Tokyo Station area" can still be closer to JR, Metro, Toei, or a private railway. The Subway Ticket is strongest when the first and last ride of the day are already on Metro or Toei.

Step 2: map the next three sightseeing rides, not the whole trip. If the first three rides are mostly Tokyo Metro and Toei, keep testing the pass. If one of them is JR Yamanote Line, Keikyu, Keisei, Tobu, Odakyu, Seibu, Tokyu, Rinkai, Yurikamome, Disney access, or an airport train, the IC card probably remains your main tool.

Step 3: count rides inside the pass clock. The tourist Subway Ticket uses 24, 48, or 72 hours from first use, while some other one-day tickets are calendar-day products. A 72-hour pass that starts on Monday afternoon may still be useful on Thursday morning, but a calendar-day ticket will not work that way.

Step 4: protect the group experience. A pass that saves a few hundred yen is not worth extra transfers with children, luggage, heat, rain, mobility limits, or a tired arrival night. If the fastest route is JR or a direct private line, pay with an IC card and keep the day simple.

Step 5: decide how you will buy and use it. Tokyo Metro says purchasers in Japan must show an actual passport proving short-term stay status, not a copy. Toei lists airport, subway station, tourist information, hotel, and retail purchase channels, but availability and ticket types should be confirmed at the sales location.

Ride-count examples that make the pass work or fail

The Tokyo Subway Ticket wins when your day is dense and station choices are flexible. A realistic winning day might be Asakusa, Ueno, Ginza, Roppongi, Shinjuku, and a hotel return, all chosen from Metro or Toei stations. A weak day might be Shibuya by JR, a long walk in Harajuku, a private-rail side trip, and one subway ride after dinner.

Do not chase savings by making the route worse. The pass should fit a plan that already works. If the pass makes you choose a slower line, change stations unnecessarily, or skip a direct ride with luggage, the small savings are probably gone.

Better alternatives for common traveler types

If you are arriving at Haneda or Narita, solve airport access first. Keikyu, Tokyo Monorail, Keisei, JR East, and limousine bus products are separate decisions. Tokyo Metro explicitly says the Tokyo Subway Ticket alone cannot be used to travel to the airport, so do not buy it as your airport-transfer plan.

If you want a visitor IC card, compare current availability. JR East describes Welcome Suica as a visitor card with no deposit and a 28-day validity period, while Welcome Suica Mobile is an iOS app option with its own country, device, and non-refund limits. PASMO's 2026 TOURIST PASMO page describes a 28-day visitor card sold at Haneda and Narita with no deposit and no refund of added value. These cards solve convenience, not fare discounts.

Common mistakes

The first mistake is treating Suica as a pass. It is a prepaid payment card, not an unlimited ride product. The second mistake is treating the Subway Ticket as an all-Tokyo pass. It excludes JR lines, airport rail, most private railways, buses outside the included subway system, and many side trips.

The third mistake is buying too early without knowing the hotel station. A pass can be excellent from one hotel and annoying from another. The fourth mistake is comparing only ticket price and ignoring time, stairs, transfers, and group energy.

The fifth mistake is forgetting cash. JR East says physical Suica top-ups are cash-only at listed machines, and JNTO warns that international cards are not always accepted for short-distance train and subway fares. Even in Tokyo, keep yen for top-ups, small restaurants, lockers, temples, and backup transport.

  • Do not use an old 800/1,200/1,500 yen price note for the Tokyo Subway Ticket without rechecking official 2026 operator pages.
  • Do not assume a mobile IC card works on every phone, country account, or card issuer.
  • Do not leave zero IC-card balance when the pass expires late at night.
  • Do not optimize for pass savings on the first jet-lagged arrival evening.
  • Do not buy a multi-day pass for days dominated by Disney, Yokohama, Kamakura, Hakone, or airport transfers.

If you only do one thing

Before buying the Tokyo Subway Ticket, map your next three rides from the hotel. If all or almost all are Tokyo Metro or Toei Subway, count rides and consider the 24, 48, or 72-hour pass. If even one core leg is JR, airport rail, private railway, bus, or an uncertain family decision, load an IC card first and make the pass a later add-on.

The cleanest first-trip setup is one IC card with enough balance, a little cash for top-ups and small payments, and a Subway Ticket only for a planned subway-heavy Tokyo block.

FAQ

Is the Tokyo Subway Ticket better than Suica?

Only on subway-heavy Tokyo days. The Tokyo Subway Ticket can save money when most rides are on Tokyo Metro and Toei Subway, while Suica or PASMO is simpler for mixed JR, private railway, bus, shopping, and airport routes.

How much is the Tokyo Subway Ticket in 2026?

Tokyo Metro and Toei pages checked on July 18, 2026 list adult prices of 1,000 yen for 24 hours, 1,500 yen for 48 hours, and 2,000 yen for 72 hours, with child prices at half those amounts.

Does the Tokyo Subway Ticket cover JR lines?

No. It covers Tokyo Metro and Toei Subway lines. JR lines, airport railways, private railways, many buses, and side trips need another ticket or an IC card payment.

Can I use the Tokyo Subway Ticket from Haneda or Narita Airport?

No. Tokyo Metro says the Subway Ticket alone cannot be used for airport travel. Use the correct airport train or bus ticket first, then start the Subway Ticket when your Tokyo subway block begins.

Do I still need Suica or PASMO if I buy the Subway Ticket?

Yes, for most trips. Keep an IC card for non-covered lines, convenience stores, vending machines, buses, late-night backup routes, and any day when the pass expires.

Is Welcome Suica or TOURIST PASMO better for visitors?

Both are visitor IC-card options, not unlimited ride passes. Welcome Suica and TOURIST PASMO avoid a deposit but have validity and refund limits, so compare current airport availability and whether you can use the remaining balance before leaving Japan.

Should I buy a 24, 48, or 72-hour Tokyo Subway Ticket?

Buy the shortest pass that matches a real subway-heavy block. The 72-hour pass is strongest when three consecutive Tokyo days use Metro or Toei repeatedly from a well-placed hotel.

What is the biggest mistake with the Tokyo Subway Ticket?

Buying it before checking the actual operators for your hotel and next rides. If the fastest route uses JR, Keikyu, Keisei, Odakyu, Tokyu, Yurikamome, Disney access, or airport rail, IC-card pay-as-you-go may be better.
