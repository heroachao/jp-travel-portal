Quick answer: set up official alerts before you need them

Before arriving in Japan, install the Japan Tourism Agency-supervised Safety Tips app, save the JNTO Japan Safe Travel page and JMA multilingual information page, and store your hotel name, address, and phone number offline. Know the urgent numbers: 110 for police and 119 for fire or ambulance. JNTO's Japan Visitor Hotline, 050-3816-2787 inside Japan, provides 24-hour support in English, Chinese, Korean, and Japanese for emergencies and general visitor assistance.

During an earthquake or severe-weather event, stay calm, protect yourself from immediate hazards, and follow instructions from hotel, station, attraction, transport, and local authorities. Do not rush outside simply because other visitors move. If a strong earthquake occurs near the coast or a tsunami warning is issued, move to higher ground or the designated evacuation location without waiting to collect luggage.

This guide is a preparation and decision framework, not a replacement for live instructions. JMA alerts, local evacuation orders, railway notices, and staff directions are time-sensitive. Use the source that controls the risk in front of you: JMA and local authorities for hazards, the operator for transport, and emergency services for urgent help.

Who this safety guide is for

Every visitor should complete the basic setup, but it matters especially for solo travelers, families, people with mobility or medical needs, coastal itineraries, mountain trips, summer travel, winter Hokkaido routes, and travelers using tight same-day flight or train connections. The aim is not to create fear. It is to remove the small information failures that make an unusual day harder.

Ten-minute setup before the trip

Install Safety Tips and allow notifications and location use that you are comfortable with. The Japan Tourism Agency says the app can provide multilingual push notifications for earthquake early warnings, tsunami warnings, special weather warnings, volcanic warnings, evacuation information, and heatstroke information. Save JMA's multilingual disaster page as a second official source and JNTO's Japan Safe Travel Information as a traveler-focused summary.

Create one offline note containing passport-copy location, insurance contact, emergency numbers, hotel name in Japanese and English, hotel phone, next destination, essential medication list, and two personal contacts. Do not store sensitive payment or identity information in a shared note. Keep a small power bank charged, and learn the evacuation route each time you check into a new hotel.

  • Enable official alerts and keep phone emergency notifications active.
  • Save 110, 119, and the JNTO Japan Visitor Hotline.
  • Store the current hotel address and phone number offline.
  • Carry essential medication, water, and a modest power reserve.
  • Check the hotel evacuation map and nearest safe assembly point.
  • Protect a transport buffer when weather could affect a flight or remote route.

What to do during and after an earthquake

Indoors, protect yourself from glass, shelves, signs, and falling objects. Follow staff and do not use an elevator during an evacuation. In a hotel, keep shoes, phone, and room key within reach at night and use the posted evacuation route if instructed. At a station, stay clear of platform edges and overhead hazards and listen for operator announcements.

Outdoors, move away from walls, roof tiles, glass, signs, and vending machines that could fall. In a crowded district, avoid pushing toward a narrow exit. On a train, remain inside unless staff direct passengers to leave; the operator knows track power, tunnels, and evacuation conditions. Near the coast, tsunami risk changes the priority: move to higher ground or an official evacuation building promptly after strong shaking or a warning.

After shaking stops, expect aftershocks and information changes. Check for injury, fire, broken glass, and unsafe exits. Use messaging rather than repeated voice calls when networks are congested, preserve battery, and confirm facts through JMA, local authorities, JNTO, NHK World, or the relevant operator. Do not travel across the city just to return to a hotel if authorities or operators advise waiting in a safe place.

Typhoon, heavy rain, heat, and snow planning

Typhoon disruption can begin before landfall and continue after the storm through wind, rain, flooding, landslides, cancelled flights, closed bridges, and railway inspections. Move nonessential outdoor plans early, not after the last transport is cancelled. Keep one safe indoor day, one food and water reserve appropriate to your lodging, and a direct operator link for the next essential train or flight.

Heavy rain can make a route unsafe even when the city center looks normal. Mountain roads, river walks, gorges, coastal paths, and landslide-prone areas require local risk checks. Summer heat alerts can make a packed walking itinerary unsafe; reduce midday exposure, use cooling locations, drink appropriately, and follow current health guidance. Winter snow and wind can delay Hokkaido, Tohoku, Hokuriku, alpine, and airport transport, so protect a final-night buffer before an international flight.

Transport disruption: decide what is essential

Separate safety from itinerary recovery. First decide whether it is safe to move. Then identify the one essential commitment: tonight's hotel, a medical need, or an international departure. Ignore optional attractions until that commitment is stable. Check the operator's official service status, not a route app that may still display a theoretical timetable.

If service is suspended, document the official notice and keep receipts for insurance or rebooking. Contact the hotel before assuming a late arrival will be accepted automatically. Avoid joining an unverified taxi queue or walking a long unfamiliar route in severe weather. A calm extra night can be cheaper and safer than forcing the original route.

Common mistakes and safer alternatives

The most common mistake is relying on a single social-media post or translation screenshot. The second is treating a transport cancellation as only a ticket problem when the same weather affects roads and taxis. The third is waiting to find hotel information until the phone battery is low. Prepare the small offline package before the trip.

What to verify on official sources

Use Safety Tips for multilingual push alerts, JMA for hazard detail, JNTO Japan Safe Travel for visitor-oriented information, and the Japan Visitor Hotline for support. Use the municipality or hotel for evacuation instructions and the exact transport operator for service status. Recheck numbers and app availability before departure because services and supported languages can change.

  • Confirm Safety Tips notifications work before leaving home.
  • Bookmark JMA multilingual weather, earthquake, tsunami, and volcano information.
  • Save 110 for urgent police and 119 for fire or ambulance.
  • Save the JNTO Japan Visitor Hotline and your insurer contact.
  • Check every new hotel evacuation route after arrival.
  • Use operator service pages before attempting a disrupted journey.

FAQ

What app should tourists use for earthquake and weather alerts in Japan?

The Japan Tourism Agency-supervised Safety Tips app provides multilingual notifications for earthquakes, tsunami, severe weather, volcanic warnings, evacuation information, and other hazards. Also bookmark JMA and JNTO official pages.

What are Japan's emergency phone numbers?

Call 110 for urgent police assistance and 119 for fire or ambulance. JNTO also operates the 24-hour Japan Visitor Hotline at 050-3816-2787 inside Japan for multilingual visitor support.

What should I do during an earthquake in a Japanese hotel?

Protect yourself from glass and falling objects, stay calm, avoid elevators during evacuation, and follow hotel instructions and the posted evacuation route. Keep shoes, phone, and room key within reach at night.

What should I do after a strong earthquake near the coast?

Move inland or to higher ground immediately if strong shaking occurs or a tsunami warning is issued. Follow official evacuation signs and local instructions, and do not delay to collect luggage.

Will trains automatically run after an earthquake or typhoon?

No. Operators may suspend service for safety checks, wind, flooding, landslide risk, or infrastructure inspection. Use the exact operator's official service page before moving.

How much emergency equipment should a tourist carry?

Carry a practical personal reserve: charged phone, modest power bank, essential medication, water, offline hotel and contact details, and weather-appropriate clothing. Follow local guidance rather than assembling an oversized kit for normal sightseeing.

Can I rely on Google Maps during a disaster in Japan?

Use it as a navigation aid, not as the authority for hazards or transport operations. Official JMA, municipality, JNTO, hotel, and operator information should control safety decisions.

Should I cancel a trip whenever a weather advisory appears?

Not automatically. Read the location and hazard, follow current official guidance, and adjust exposed activities. A warning, emergency warning, evacuation instruction, or operator suspension requires a stronger response.
