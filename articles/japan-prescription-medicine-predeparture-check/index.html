Quick answer: yes, but check the ingredient and amount

You can bring many medicines to Japan for your own use, but the answer changes by active ingredient, quantity, dosage form, and whether the medicine is controlled under Japanese law. Do not rely on the brand name alone. Make a list of every prescription drug, over-the-counter medicine, supplement, injectable, syringe, device, and contact lens supply before you pack.

MHLW guidance checked on 2026-07-17 says some personal-use medicines can enter without Import Confirmation within stated amounts: up to a one-month supply for prescription drugs, poisons, deadly poisons, pre-filled syringes, and self-injection kits; 24 per item for many external-use drugs; and a two-month supply for other drugs and quasi-drugs. Cosmetics, household medical devices, and disposable contact lenses have separate limits.

That rule does not cover controlled or prohibited substances. The safest plan is to identify the active ingredient first, check MHLW and the Narcotics Control Department, then apply early if Import Confirmation or controlled-substance permission is needed.

Who this guide is for

This guide is for travelers who need a practical predeparture decision, not a legal encyclopedia. It is most useful if you take daily prescription medicine, ADHD medication, anxiety medication, pain medicine, sleep medicine, blood pressure medication, thyroid medication, allergy medicine, injectable medicine, or several over-the-counter products for a long trip.

It is also for parents packing medicine for children, travelers with chronic conditions, and anyone tempted to put all medicine in checked luggage. If a medicine appears on a controlled list, exceeds the ordinary personal-use amount, has an unclear active ingredient, or cannot be interrupted, contact the relevant MHLW or Narcotics Control Department office before departure.

Decision table: no procedure, Import Confirmation, or controlled-substance permission

Separate three questions: is the item under the personal-use amount, does it contain a controlled or prohibited substance, and are you carrying an unusual dosage form such as an injection? If the answer is unclear, do not guess from forums. Japan's controlled list is ingredient-based, so the active ingredient matters more than the brand name.

Step-by-step predeparture plan

Step 1: make a medicine inventory. For each item, write the brand name, active ingredient, strength, dosage, quantity, dosage form, prescribing doctor, and whether you need it during the flight. Include cold medicine, sleep aids, painkillers, patches, liquids, injections, syringes, supplements, contact lenses, and devices.

Step 2: classify each item against official Japan sources. Use MHLW for general personal-use medicine and Import Confirmation, and use the Narcotics Control Department for controlled substances. Japan Customs frames private import as personal use only.

Step 3: check the quantity rule. A prescription drug above one month, an ordinary drug above two months, or an item outside a simple personal-use category belongs in the apply-or-ask group.

Step 4: prepare documents. Carry original packaging, the prescription label, a prescription copy, and a doctor letter listing your name, treatment reason, medicine names, doses, strengths, and doctor signature. NCD guidance says controlled-substance medical certificates need a specific treatment reason, not only for travel or personal use.

Step 5: keep essential medicine and documents in your carry-on. If you receive Import Confirmation or a controlled-substance certificate, keep it where you can show it on arrival. Recheck official pages close to departure because lists, contacts, and application details can change.

High-risk examples travelers ask about

ADHD medication needs special caution. NCD guidance checked on 2026-07-17 says amphetamine, the active ingredient of Adderall, cannot be imported even for treatment, while lisdexamfetamine products such as Vyvanse require advance permission.

Pain medicine, sleep medicine, anxiety medicine, and cold medicine also need ingredient-level checking. NCD examples include narcotics such as morphine, fentanyl, oxycodone, codeine, and tapentadol, and stimulant raw materials such as pseudoephedrine. Do not decide from whether the medicine is legally prescribed at home.

Alternatives when the medicine is restricted

If your medicine cannot enter Japan, do not hide it, mail it, or ask another person to carry it. Ask your prescribing doctor whether a Japan-legal alternative exists or whether the treatment plan can be adjusted safely. For medicines that can enter with permission, build the application timeline into the trip plan.

If you need a longer supply, use the official Import Confirmation route rather than packing excess medicine and hoping it is ignored. If you become sick in Japan, rely on insurance and medical-support planning as the backup. JNTO advises travelers to understand insurance payment, emergency options, and language support.

Common mistakes

The biggest mistake is searching only the brand name. Official Japan pages organize rules by legal category and active ingredient, so a familiar home-country brand can still be a problem in Japan.

The second mistake is packing more than you need. Personal-use exceptions are quantity-limited, and extra backup medicine can trigger an Import Confirmation question. The third mistake is assuming a prescription always solves the issue; NCD warns that some medicines cannot be imported even if prescribed abroad.

The fourth mistake is mailing medicine to Japan or handing it to a family member. NCD guidance for narcotics and stimulant raw materials says the traveler must carry the medicine personally. The fifth mistake is leaving documents in checked luggage.

  • Do not bring prohibited substances because they are legal at home.
  • Do not rely on Reddit, hotel staff, or airline check-in staff for a controlled-drug answer.
  • Do not split medicine across bags to hide the quantity.
  • Do not remove labels from prescription packaging.
  • Do not wait until the final week if NCD permission may be needed.

FAQ

Can I bring prescription medication to Japan?

Often yes for personal use, but only after checking the active ingredient, quantity, and dosage form. MHLW says prescription drugs within a one-month supply may not need Import Confirmation, but controlled substances and larger quantities can require separate procedures.

Can I bring ADHD medication to Japan?

You must check the active ingredient with the Narcotics Control Department. NCD says Adderall's amphetamine ingredient cannot be imported even for treatment, while lisdexamfetamine products such as Vyvanse require advance permission.

Do I need a Yunyu Kakunin-sho or Import Confirmation?

You may need Import Confirmation when you exceed ordinary personal-use amounts or bring items that do not fit the no-procedure categories. Use the official MHLW guidance and online application rather than guessing from another traveler's case.

How much medicine can I bring to Japan without Import Confirmation?

MHLW guidance checked on 2026-07-17 lists up to a one-month supply for prescription drugs and certain injections, up to 24 per item for many external-use drugs, and up to a two-month supply for other drugs and quasi-drugs. Controlled substances can have different rules.

Can I bring over-the-counter cold medicine to Japan?

Maybe, but check the active ingredients. Some ordinary-looking cold medicines contain ingredients that can be controlled or treated differently in Japan, so do not pack extra products without checking MHLW and NCD guidance.

Should medicine go in checked luggage or carry-on?

Keep essential medicine and all permission documents in your carry-on. Checked bags can be delayed, and documents are harder to show if they are separated from the medicine.

Can someone else carry my controlled medicine into Japan?

For narcotics and stimulant raw materials, NCD says the traveler must carry the medicine personally and cannot send it or entrust carrying it to another person. Check the official guidance for your category.

What documents should I bring for medication in Japan?

Carry original packaging, prescription labels, a copy of the prescription, and a doctor letter when relevant. For controlled-substance applications, NCD lists specific medical certificate details, including medicine names, doses, strengths, treatment reason, doctor's signature, and issue date.
