Tokyo First-Time Neighborhood Planner

A practical way to choose Tokyo bases and daily routes by grouping neighborhoods, rail lines, and travel pace.

Published June 18, 2026 Updated June 18, 2026 2 min read GO TOKYO: The Official Tokyo Travel Guide

Start with rail geography, not a checklist

Tokyo is easier when a first trip is planned around clusters rather than isolated landmarks. Put one or two neighborhoods into each day, then use the nearest rail or subway connection as the backbone.

For most visitors, a central base near a major station reduces transfers and makes late-day changes less stressful. Tokyo Station, Shinjuku, Ueno, Shibuya, and Ginza all work differently; the right choice depends on whether the trip is focused on food, shopping, museums, day trips, or nightlife.

Suggested first-time grouping

Use west Tokyo for Shinjuku, Shibuya, Harajuku, and modern shopping. Use east Tokyo for Ueno, Asakusa, museums, older streets, and river areas. Keep Ginza, Marunouchi, and Tokyo Station together for a polished central day.

  • Two or three full Tokyo days: pick one west-side day, one east-side day, and one flexible central day.
  • Four or five days: add a food-focused evening, a garden or museum morning, and a day trip buffer.
  • Families and first arrivals should avoid changing hotels inside Tokyo unless there is a strong reason.

Practical travel note

IC cards are useful for many rail and bus services in Tokyo and other parts of Japan. They are prepaid and rechargeable, which makes small movements across the city simpler than buying a paper ticket each time.

FAQ

Where should a first-time visitor stay in Tokyo?

Choose a base with easy rail access. Shinjuku and Shibuya suit nightlife and shopping; Tokyo Station and Ginza suit central convenience; Ueno and Asakusa suit museums, older streets, and better-value stays.

How many Tokyo neighborhoods should I visit in one day?

Two main neighborhoods plus one nearby evening area is a realistic first-trip pace.
