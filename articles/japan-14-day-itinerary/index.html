Quick answer: four bases and thirteen nights

For a first two-week Japan trip, use Tokyo for four nights, Kyoto for four, Hiroshima for two and Osaka for three, in that order. Arrive on Day 1 and depart Kansai on Day 14. You get contrasting city neighborhoods, two substantial Kyoto days, a choice of Nara or Uji, and a Hiroshima/Miyajima block without sleeping somewhere new every evening.

If your return flight leaves Tokyo, use Osaka for two nights and return to Tokyo for Night 13. Do not travel from Hiroshima to your international flight on the final morning. If fewer hotel moves matter more than western Japan, remove Hiroshima and redistribute its nights. A good two-week itinerary is a set of worthwhile days, not a requirement to cover the country.

Who this guide is for

Use this route if you have thirteen hotel nights, want a first visit centered on cities and cultural sights, and are comfortable with several intercity rail moves. It works particularly well when you want time in Hiroshima rather than a rushed visit from Kansai. Four bases allow evenings and recovery days in each region, but they still mean packing and navigating stations.

Families can shorten walking days and keep the same allocation. Travelers with mobility needs should select accessible hotels and routes first; an area recommendation is not an accessibility guarantee. This is not the right starting point for a skiing holiday, an Okinawa island trip or a rural driving itinerary. Replace a substantial block if those are the reason for traveling.

Days 1-4: Tokyo with one genuinely flexible day

Day 1: arrival is a complete travel task

Have the hotel address and airport route available before landing. After immigration and baggage collection, confirm which service still fits the real time. A Narita ticket does not solve a Haneda arrival, and the fastest train to one station may not be the easiest way to a different hotel. Start with our Narita or Haneda comparison.

Check in, eat nearby and get the essentials ready for tomorrow. Save the first big reservation for a later day. A short evening stroll is useful if you feel awake, but sleep is more valuable than a long cross-city detour after a long-haul flight.

Days 2 and 3: separate east and west

Use Day 2 for Asakusa's Sensoji area, followed by one Ueno-area museum or park afternoon. Those places form an east-side day; choose transport between them rather than assuming every stop is adjacent. A closed museum is a reason to change the order, not to fill the gap with four other districts. If crafts or kitchenware matter more, replace the museum with a focused shopping visit.

Use Day 3 for Meiji Jingu and Harajuku, then Shibuya. Pick a main purpose in each area instead of collecting shopping streets. If a viewpoint is important, book through the venue and organize the afternoon around that slot. Do not count on perfect sunset conditions. If your interests lean toward Shinjuku, substitute it for one of these blocks rather than making a second late evening compulsory.

These two days can swap. Use the better weather for the outdoor emphasis and respect each operator's calendar. Our Tokyo recovery itinerary gives more detail without requiring you to do every suggestion.

Day 4: choose one priority and prepare for the move

Reserve this day for the experience you would regret missing: a museum, a neighborhood food day, a theme park or a carefully selected excursion. A theme park is the day's main activity. Do not combine its entry with a morning across Tokyo and a fixed dinner elsewhere. Bookings, return transport and energy need to fit together.

Kamakura can replace the Tokyo day if coastal walks and temples appeal and the forecast suits. Use the Kamakura day-trip guide to decide how much walking to include. Avoid adding both Hakone and Kamakura merely because each is reachable: you would be using the same flexible time twice.

If you stay in Tokyo, do laundry and pack before a relaxed dinner. Fourteen days creates a real need for clean clothes, not fourteen separate outfits in a larger bag. Check the hotel's facilities and payment method before relying on them. A recovery afternoon helps the Kyoto block start well.

Days 5-8: Kyoto with room for one nearby outing

Day 5: transfer and choose a small evening

Take the booked Tokyo- or Shinagawa-to-Kyoto service that fits your checkout and station route. Tickets, luggage and seat arrangements belong to the booking stage. Keep the Kyoto hotel handoff separate from sightseeing. A room may not be ready when the train arrives; bag storage depends on the property's agreement.

A station-area meal or a downtown walk is enough after settling in. Do not place a nonrefundable cultural activity immediately after the train just because the nominal journey looks short. The Kyoto hotel-area guide helps balance train access against evenings and the districts you actually plan to visit.

Day 6: southern Higashiyama

Make Kiyomizu-dera the day's main temple if its setting is your priority. Check its official access information and admission arrangements; the hillside approach deserves time and suitable footwear. Continue through the Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka area at a pace that leaves room for lunch. These are not empty photography sets.

Yasaka Shrine and a public Gion-area walk can follow if energy permits. Obey private-access and photography notices and leave working people space. Avoid turning the afternoon into a cross-city attempt to reach Arashiyama. Keep that district for its own day. If a traveler cannot manage the hills, use a more accessible cultural visit whose facilities you have confirmed.

Day 7: Arashiyama or another district

Choose a route to Arashiyama from the hotel, then select one principal garden or temple visit and a riverside walk. The bamboo path is only one part of the area. A scenic train or boat activity needs its own ticket and operating check; it is not automatically included in an ordinary rail journey to Arashiyama.

If this district is not a priority, use northern or central Kyoto instead. Do not construct a route that requires several long bus rides merely to visit the famous stops. Kyoto's official guidance recommends combining modes and avoiding sightseeing with large luggage. Our Kyoto transport guide translates that into a workable day.

Day 8: choose Nara, Uji or more Kyoto

For Nara, decide between JR and Kintetsu using both hotel access and the walking route on arrival. The two Nara stations are different places. The official Nara guide places Kintetsu Nara closer to the park; this does not make every Kintetsu service the best choice from every hotel. Pick one heritage priority, leave space for lunch and treat the deer as wild animals.

For a shorter outing, choose Uji's tea and Byodoin area, checking venue operations before the day. If you feel worn out, remain in Kyoto for a craft visit or a quiet neighborhood afternoon. See the Nara route guide or Uji half-day guide. Do not combine them with a full Arashiyama day.

Pack for Hiroshima that evening. Confirm whether the main bag is traveling with you or being sent to a later hotel. A small overnight bag for two Hiroshima nights can simplify the western block, but only after the carrier and Osaka property have confirmed delivery and acceptance.

Days 9-10: give Hiroshima and Miyajima separate space

Day 9: travel west, then make one meaningful city visit

Use a confirmed Kyoto-to-Hiroshima rail itinerary. Depending on the service and ticket, it may involve a change; do not assume every departure is direct. On arrival, leave bags at the agreed hotel or a confirmed storage option. Hiroshima Station and the Peace Memorial Park area are not the same location, so include the local journey in the afternoon.

Give the Peace Memorial Park and museum time and attention. The museum's material can be emotionally difficult, particularly for children. Check current admission and reservation requirements with the museum before selecting the day's train. This guide does not reproduce an unverified entrance slot or guarantee walk-in admission.

If arrival timing leaves too little room, use a respectful park visit and reserve the museum for Day 10, reducing or dropping Miyajima. That is a better adjustment than racing through both. Keep the evening modest: a meal and some time to reflect are worthwhile parts of the day. Do not attach a long list of extra city attractions to an already substantial visit.

Day 10: Miyajima as a full-day choice

Check the ferry operator's service notices before leaving. One rail option is the JR Sanyo Main Line from Hiroshima to Miyajimaguchi, followed by the walk to the JR ferry terminal. The railway station is on the mainland; reaching it is not the same as arriving on the island. Allow for walking, ticket handling, waiting and the ferry rather than quoting the train ride as the whole journey.

Make the waterfront and Itsukushima Shrine area your priority, subject to the shrine's current operations. A view of the torii at a particular tide is a separate planning choice. Consult a dated local tide source if that matters, and do not promise that the gate will appear to float at your chosen arrival time. Have lunch before choosing another activity.

A Mount Misen hike or ropeway visit needs its own weather, operating and return assessment. It should replace a substantial part of the island day, not be added just before the final ferry. Groups with limited mobility can choose a shorter waterfront plan and the operator's barrier-free information. Return with room for an earlier service rather than relying on the last possible connection.

The JR ferry's fare page separately lists a JPY 100 Miyajima Visitor Tax on island entry, with exemptions. Eligible rail-pass ferry coverage does not make that tax disappear. Recheck the current fare and tax arrangement before boarding. Do not assume a pass accepted by the JR ferry is valid with every other boat operator.

If ferries or weather disrupt the plan, stay on the mainland and use the Hiroshima day you have kept available. Two nights give you options, not immunity from disruption. Use the Hiroshima and Miyajima guide for the more detailed local decision.

Days 11-14: Osaka, then the departure airport

Day 11: return to Kansai and settle into the right district

Travel from Hiroshima to Shin-Osaka using the confirmed ticket. Shin-Osaka is the Shinkansen station, not JR Osaka Station and not Namba. Add the final local journey to your hotel. A Umeda stay can use its own dinner options; a Namba stay can enjoy Minami without first crossing the city again.

This is an arrival day, not a full Osaka sightseeing day. Choose one nearby evening walk after check-in. The Osaka accommodation guide compares the airport, station, room and evening tradeoffs. The Shin-Osaka transfer guide is useful for the final connection.

Day 12: one Osaka priority or a deliberate outing

Choose a city interest such as the Osaka Castle area, a food-focused Minami day or a checked indoor attraction. If Universal Studios Japan is essential, treat it as the full day's commitment and verify tickets through the operator. Do not combine it with an early Himeji excursion and expect every evening plan to survive.

For a day trip, compare the hotel-to-first-train journey and the evening return before choosing the destination. An Umeda-area base may serve several northern and western routes conveniently, but the exact station and train still matter. Nara need not appear twice if you already visited it from Kyoto. Use Osaka's own neighborhoods instead of repeating a destination for the sake of another itinerary label.

Day 13: protect the last night

Keep this day for a missed local priority, manageable shopping and packing. Check the terminal, airport arrival requirement and the first usable transport for tomorrow. Shopping should end early enough to organize baggage; a larger suitcase may change the airport or rail plan. Keep money and essential travel documents separate from checked or forwarded bags.

For Tokyo departure, travel back today and remove the Osaka sightseeing block. For a very early Kansai flight, replace the city hotel with a property whose confirmed transfer reaches the correct terminal in time. Do not assume every airport hotel shuttle operates overnight.

Day 14: leave from the region where you slept

Take the airport route selected for your property and flight. Nankai serves the Namba side; JR options serve locations including Tennoji, Osaka and Shin-Osaka. Kansai Airport is not downtown Osaka. Verify service and terminal access that morning, and use the Kansai airport guide for the detailed comparison.

Rail-pass comparison: put the expensive journeys on a calendar

In the default route, the main intercity journeys fall on Days 5, 9 and 11. Those seven calendar days may make a seven-day pass worth comparing even though the holiday lasts fourteen days. This is a comparison window, not a conclusion that the pass saves money. A Tokyo return on Day 13 lies outside a Day 5-11 window.

Hotel, luggage and budget worksheet

Book Tokyo for arrival and city access; Kyoto for the districts and evenings you want; Hiroshima for a manageable city visit and ferry day; Osaka for the final activities and airport route. Confirm the room's permitted occupants, cancellation conditions and baggage acceptance. The lowest nightly rate is not automatically the lowest-cost route.

For Shinkansen legs, measure bags and check the relevant carrier guidance. JR Central requires an appropriate oversized-baggage reservation for ordinary luggage totaling more than 160 cm and up to 250 cm on the relevant services. For forwarding, confirm delivery timing with the carrier, not the itinerary. Keep enough essentials for any nights before the bag arrives.

What to verify on official sources

Before paying, check the exact rail product, permitted room occupants, hotel cancellation rules, museum admission process and any essential activity. For Miyajima, check the ferry operator, island-entry tax, shrine information and any ropeway or hiking notices. Before travel days, recheck service disruptions and weather. Neither a source link nor a booking screen viewed months ago guarantees future operation.

Keep the hotel address, ticket details, airport terminal and the relevant operator pages available on your phone. A fourteen-day trip needs these practical records repeatedly. Use the route as a stable framework and revise optional activities as conditions change, without silently changing the total nights.

FAQ

How many nights should I book for fourteen days in Japan?

If arrival is Day 1 and departure is Day 14, book thirteen nights. This route uses Tokyo four, Kyoto four, Hiroshima two and Osaka three. For Tokyo departure, replace the final Osaka night with Tokyo, keeping thirteen total.

Why does Hiroshima come before the final Osaka stay?

Returning to Kansai before the final three nights keeps the international flight day separate from a long western-Japan rail journey. It also gives Osaka evenings and a flexible day before departure.

Should I buy a fourteen-day JR Pass?

Compare rather than assume. The main intercity journeys in this route are Days 5, 9 and 11, so a seven-day window is one scenario to test. A Tokyo return on Day 13 lies outside it. Include purchase-channel prices, supplements and exclusions.

Can I remove Hiroshima or add Hakone?

Yes, by reallocating existing nights. The slower version uses Tokyo five, Kyoto five and Osaka three. The Hakone version replaces Tokyo Night 4, leaving Tokyo three, Hakone one, Kyoto four, Hiroshima two and Osaka three.

Can I visit Hiroshima and Miyajima in one day?

It is not the recommended version of this route. Two Hiroshima nights give separate space for the city and an island day. If arrival or weather reduces usable time, prioritize the meaningful city visit and shorten or remove Miyajima.

What should I do if my return flight leaves Tokyo?

Use Osaka for Nights 11 and 12, travel to Tokyo on Day 13 and sleep there. Remove the flexible Osaka day rather than putting it before the train. Check the final hotel against the actual airport and first usable connection.
