Quick answer: budget by category, not by averages

A useful Japan travel budget is a category plan, not a borrowed daily average. Start with hotels, long-distance rail, local transport, airport transfers, food, paid activities, shopping, luggage, laundry, taxes, and reserve money. Then attach each category to your actual route, because a Tokyo-only trip, a Tokyo-Kyoto-Osaka first trip, and a Kansai plus Hiroshima route have different cost pressure.

Official pages checked on 2026-07-19 confirm several fixed or semi-fixed inputs. The Japan Rail Pass official price page lists ordinary adult online prices of JPY 50,000 for 7 days, JPY 80,000 for 14 days, and JPY 100,000 for 21 days, with overseas agency prices changing for purchases made on or after 2026-10-01. Tokyo Metro lists Tokyo Subway Ticket adult prices of JPY 1,000, JPY 1,500, and JPY 2,000 for 24, 48, and 72 hours. Japan's International Tourist Tax is JPY 3,000 per departure from Japan from 2026-07-01, with a transitional measure for eligible tickets issued on or before 2026-06-30.

Those facts do not create a complete budget. Hotel rates, restaurant spending, event tickets, exchange rates, pass value, luggage forwarding, theme parks, and shopping totals still depend on date, route, room size, booking channel, and traveler style. The goal is to separate fixed official checks from variable live checks before you pay.

Who this budget planner is for

Use this page if you are planning a first or second Japan trip and need to know where the money actually goes. It is useful before booking hotels, buying a JR Pass, adding day trips, or deciding whether a trip is budget, moderate, or comfort-focused. It is also useful for families and groups because per-person averages often hide room size, taxis, breakfast, luggage, and paid attraction differences.

This is not a live quote and does not replace official railway pages, hotel booking pages, attraction ticket pages, tax agency guidance, or your bank exchange rate. It gives you a defensible order of operations so that the largest costs are checked before smaller daily spending fills the spreadsheet.

Official cost checks for 2026 planning

Use official numbers only where official numbers exist. On 2026-07-19, the Japan Rail Pass official page listed ordinary adult online prices of JPY 50,000, JPY 80,000, and JPY 100,000 for 7, 14, and 21 days. It also listed higher ordinary adult overseas agency prices for purchases made on or after 2026-10-01: JPY 53,000, JPY 84,000, and JPY 105,000. That does not mean you should buy a pass; it means the pass row should be checked against exact route legs before purchase.

For local Tokyo days, Tokyo Metro listed Tokyo Subway Ticket prices of JPY 1,000 for 24 hours, JPY 1,500 for 48 hours, and JPY 2,000 for 72 hours for adults, valid on Tokyo Metro and Toei Subway lines. This can be good value on subway-heavy days, but it does not cover every JR, private railway, airport, bus, or walking-heavy plan.

For departure tax, official Japan Tourism Agency and National Tax Agency materials state that the International Tourist Tax is JPY 3,000 per departure from Japan from 2026-07-01, with transitional treatment for eligible tickets issued on or before 2026-06-30 and an exemption for infants under age 2. Airlines and sea carriers generally collect it through the ticket price, so travelers often experience it as part of the fare rather than a separate counter payment.

For shopping, the Japan tax-free official site states that purchases made on or after 2026-11-01 shift to a refund method: travelers buy tax-free goods at tax-inclusive prices, then the amount equivalent to consumption tax is refunded after customs inspection confirms the goods are taken out of Japan. If your trip crosses that date, do not rely on old point-of-sale exemption habits.

Accommodation tax and hotel-base cost

Accommodation tax is small compared with hotel rates, but it matters because it is easy to forget and differs by city. Tokyo Metropolitan Government guidance checked on 2026-07-19 lists JPY 100 per person per night for room charges from JPY 10,000 to less than JPY 15,000, JPY 200 for JPY 15,000 or more, and no tax below JPY 10,000. The same page notes a different Tokyo system from 2027-04-01, so future trips need a fresh check.

Kyoto City lists a per-person, per-night accommodation tax from JPY 200 under JPY 6,000 to JPY 10,000 at JPY 100,000 or more, with intermediate bands of JPY 400, JPY 1,000, and JPY 4,000. Osaka Prefecture lists the revised rates as no tax below JPY 5,000, then JPY 200, JPY 400, and JPY 500 by price band. Use the official local page for the city where you sleep; do not assume Tokyo, Kyoto, and Osaka match.

The larger hotel-budget issue is base quality. A cheaper hotel outside the daily route can create repeated local fares, taxis, luggage stress, and lost time. A slightly more expensive station-friendly base can be cheaper in the real trip if it reduces transfers, late-night rides, and baggage friction.

Step-by-step budget action plan

Step 1: write the route as nights, not attractions. For example, Tokyo four nights, Kyoto three nights, Osaka two nights, and one airport or final-city night is easier to budget than a list of places. Step 2: choose hotel bases by route value and final walking comfort, then price the room total for the whole group.

Step 3: list long-distance rail legs before buying any pass. Include Tokyo to Kyoto or Shin-Osaka, any Hiroshima, Hokuriku, Tohoku, Kyushu, or airport rail legs, and whether an open-jaw flight removes a long return. Step 4: decide local transport by actual days. A Tokyo Subway Ticket can be useful on subway-heavy days, while an IC-card-first plan can be cleaner for mixed JR, private rail, bus, and walking days.

Step 5: give food more than one line. Separate convenience-store breakfasts, casual lunches, market snacks, special dinners, theme-park meals, ryokan meals, and coffee breaks. Step 6: price paid activities individually. A theme park, observation deck, museum, festival seat, or tour should not hide inside a generic daily allowance.

Step 7: add shopping, tax-free timing, extra baggage, lockers, laundry, luggage forwarding, taxis, medicine, and reserve money. Step 8: run one bad-day test. If rain, a delayed arrival, a full locker, or tired travelers force a taxi and a simple meal, the budget should still survive without stealing from the final hotel or airport transfer.

Common mistakes

The first mistake is using someone else's daily average without matching their route, dates, group size, room type, rail legs, and shopping behavior. The second mistake is treating the JR Pass as a default Japan cost. The pass price is official, but the value is route-specific and should be checked against real train legs.

The third mistake is budgeting hotels without local taxes and final transport. A hotel can look cheaper until you add accommodation tax, a difficult station walk, repeated subway rides, or a taxi on transfer days. The fourth mistake is hiding major activities inside food money. A theme park day and a convenience-store day are not the same category.

The fifth mistake is spending the reserve before the trip starts. Reserve money is for disruption: weather, illness, missed trains, emergency taxis, replacement clothes, lockers, laundry, luggage forwarding, and airport time. The sixth mistake is using old tax-free shopping advice for a trip after 2026-11-01, when the refund method changes the cash-flow and customs-timing plan.

What to verify on official sources

Verify the official JR Pass price and purchase-channel rules before buying. Verify local rail or subway ticket prices on operator pages, not old blog tables. Verify International Tourist Tax rules if your ticket date or departure date is near a change or transitional measure. Verify tax-free shopping rules on the official Japan tax-free site before building a shopping-heavy itinerary.

For hotel taxes, verify Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka, or the local government page for the exact city where you sleep. For attractions, verify official ticket prices, closure days, timed-entry rules, and cancellation policies directly on the attraction or event page. For exchange rates and card fees, verify with your bank or card issuer because travel articles cannot know your settlement rate.

  • Check the current JR Pass price page and the route value before purchasing.
  • Check Tokyo Metro or local operators before choosing a subway or IC-card strategy.
  • Check city accommodation tax pages after choosing hotel cities and room price bands.
  • Check International Tourist Tax rules for your ticket and departure date.
  • Check tax-free shopping rules if purchases will happen on or after 2026-11-01.
  • Check official attraction pages for live prices and closure days.

If you only do one thing

Build a one-page budget with eight rows before booking: hotels and lodging tax, long-distance rail, local transport, airport transfers, food, activities, shopping, and reserve money. Put official checked facts beside the rows where official facts exist, and put a live-check note beside the rows that change by date.

Then stress-test the route. If the budget only works when every train is perfect, every hotel is cheap, nobody needs a taxi, and nobody shops, it is not a real budget. Improve the hotel base, remove one paid anchor, shorten one rail add-on, or increase the reserve before paying.

FAQ

How should I start a Japan travel budget?

Start with route nights, hotel bases, and long-distance rail legs. Then add local transport, airport transfers, food, activities, shopping, taxes, luggage, laundry, and reserve money as separate rows.

Should I buy a JR Pass for budget reasons?

Only after pricing exact eligible rail legs. The official pass price is easy to check, but whether it saves money depends on route, dates, train choices, purchase channel, and whether the trip has an open-jaw flight.

How do hotel taxes affect a Japan budget?

They vary by city and room price band. Check the official local page for each city where you sleep, then add the tax to the total stay cost rather than treating it as a surprise at the front desk.

Is the Tokyo Subway Ticket always cheaper than IC-card use?

No. Tokyo Metro lists the ticket prices, but value depends on whether your actual day uses Tokyo Metro and Toei Subway heavily. Mixed JR, private railway, bus, or walking days may be simpler with normal IC-card use.

What is the International Tourist Tax in 2026?

Official tourism and tax materials checked on 2026-07-19 state that the tax is JPY 3,000 per departure from Japan from 2026-07-01 in principle, with transitional treatment for eligible tickets issued earlier and an infant exemption.

How does the November 2026 tax-free change affect travelers?

For purchases from 2026-11-01, the official tax-free site says Japan shifts to a refund method after customs inspection confirms goods are taken out. Budget for paying tax-inclusive prices first and protecting airport time.

How much reserve money should I keep?

Set a reserve that is not already assigned to food or shopping. It should cover disruption such as weather, taxis, lockers, laundry, luggage forwarding, missed trains, medicine, and airport changes.

When should I update the budget?

Update it after flights, after hotels, after rail legs, before buying attraction tickets, and again shortly before departure because official fares, taxes, closures, exchange rates, and ticket rules can change.
